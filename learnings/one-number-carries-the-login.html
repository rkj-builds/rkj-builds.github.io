Our one WhatsApp number also carries the login

I said Meta suppresses messages to anyone without an opt-in. Rahul asked how, then, every brand he never opted into reaches him. He was right.

Where
WhatsApp, parents of the kids' programme
Cost
A wrong belief about opt-in, and our own gate blocking parents we had every right to message
The rule
The load-bearing number never sends marketing. Split the numbers before the first promotional send, not after.

What happened

Nothing at Meta's end checks opt-in at send time. An approved template goes to any number. Enforcement is reactive: blocks and reports lower the number's quality rating, then templates get paused and the number gets restricted.

The gate that was actually blocking us was ours. A rule written for cold marketing to a booking list refused any phone without a preferences row, and it was being applied to parents who had given us their number to enrol a child.

What it taught us

The asymmetry that decides everything: the venue has one WhatsApp number, and it sends the dashboard's login codes. A quality restriction would not just stop marketing. It would log the team out of their own business. Brands that spam have millions of disposable numbers. We have one, and it is load-bearing.

The rule now

Enrolment is the basis for utility messages, recorded with its date and source. Parent messages and broadcasts go from a second number and refuse the main one. The split was made before the first promotional send, because a restricted number cannot be un-restricted by deciding to be careful afterwards.
