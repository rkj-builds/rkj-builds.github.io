A customer paid ₹350, was moved, and was asked for ₹350 again

The money existed in the database. It sat on a cancelled row, and nothing could see it or spend it.

Where
Court bookings, payments
Cost
One customer asked to pay twice; a design we should have had from day one
The rule
Money on a cancelled booking becomes a credit at once, and the credit comes off before any new ask.

What happened

A booking was rescheduled. The old row was cancelled, the new row was created, and the new row asked for its price. The ₹350 already taken was attached to the cancelled row, correct and useless. The customer was asked again.

What it taught us

Cancelling is not the end of the money. It is the start of a debt the venue owes. So the order matters and it is deliberate: reschedule keeps the booking and its money; cancelling issues a credit immediately and says so; "Refund instead" is a button under that message, a second act, never the first offer.

The rules live in the database, not in the code that calls it: one credit per cancelled booking, never overdrawn, expiry stamped at issue so a later settings change cannot shorten a credit someone already holds. The first version of "never overdrawn" gave up on contention instead of re-reading; a test caught it.

The rule now

A credit comes off before any payment ask, and the payment rail and the message to the customer carry the same reduced number, because a request that does not match what was taken is a dispute. Refunds cap against the gateway's own refunded amount and refuse an over-ask rather than trimming it. A refund that times out does not restore the credit, because the gateway may have taken it; a person looks.
