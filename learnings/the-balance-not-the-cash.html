The number to collect is the ledger's balance, not the cash in hand

Asked how much the coach owed the venue, I reported ₹35,983. The real figure was ₹5,237, and it had been settled a week earlier.

Where
Off-Screen Club, the money split with the coach
Cost
One wrong figure, caught before it reached anyone
The rule
A settlement question is answered by the ledger, never by adding up who holds what.

What happened

The kids' programme runs on a split: the coach keeps 65% of what he collects and owes the venue 35%, and the venue owes him 65% of what it collects. Settlement happens roughly weekly, in a WhatsApp chat, in cash and UPI.

On 20 October I summed every rupee the coach had held since 3 October and called it "to collect". That number was wrong twice. It ignored the 35% split, and it ignored the settlement he had made on 11 October. Rahul corrected it from memory, which is the only reason it was caught.

What it taught us

Gross cash held is a fact about custody. What someone owes is a fact about the ledger: every collection on both sides, the split applied, minus every settlement so far. The two numbers look alike and are never equal.

The second lesson is that a settlement which lives only in a chat does not exist to the software. Until it is recorded, every balance the desk shows is stale.

The rule now

The desk has a third card on the register: the balance between the two accounts, computed from the event accounts, with a "Settle up to" action that records a settlement through a date. The question "how much does he owe" is answered by that card and nothing else.
