The brief describes Quark's page. The audience is not Quark's.

The ticket asks for a hero with an embedded lead form, modelled on Quark Expeditions' PPC page, with nine confirmed HubSpot fields and a WordPress build that posts to HubSpot. The attached wireframes put name, email and phone at the top of the form and add a budget field the form does not have.

The traffic that will land here is known. Two Meta top-of-funnel campaigns carry more than half of paid users, at 24 to 32% engagement; 82% of paid sessions are on a phone; and on the page these users already reach, they page photos and tap the quote builder, not the phone number. A contact-first form on a split hero fights all three behaviours at once.

The one constraint that shaped the design: every one of the nine HubSpot fields has to be posted with its existing value, and leads have to carry a Paid source so marketing can report them.

Hypothesis

If the hero keeps the photo and asks the three trip questions before any personal detail, then lead submissions per session will be higher than a nine-field contact-first form, because every comparable form orders it this way and 15% of mobile taps already go to a trip-question flow on the page these users reach today.

Recommended for the first page: the converged hero with the price on

The phone layout is not optional: 82% of paid sessions are phones and photos are their first action, so the gallery, the chips and the sheet are the base. The trip-first order is common to every comparable form. The one real choice is the price. Showing it is where the evidence points: shared quotes are 57% of sales-qualified leads, 15% of mobile taps already chase a price, and two of three charter competitors put a price before the form. Quark hides its price because it has none to show; The Moorings has one on the destination page today. Go live with the price toggle on. If the departures endpoint is not wired by launch, ship with it off and make "price on" the first A/B test, because it is the biggest bet with the clearest mechanism.

Spacing pass after review (30 Sep): the form stack moved from the page's 30px column gutter to a 16px rhythm, visible labels shortened to match the chips, the card heading and button stepped down one size, and the hero's vertical padding reduced. The card went from about 600px to 433px tall, so hero, card and proof strip fit an 800px viewport; step 2 opens as a pop-up, and its submit button is a sticky footer with a fade above it, so long fields scroll under the button on short phones. The design system's form group still uses the 30px gutter for stacked fields; flagged for promotion.

What happens when it goes wrong

One hero, any destination

The team will run paid pages for more than the BVI. The hero is a single WordPress block driven by a base: pick the destination, paste the ad headline, choose the photos, leave the price toggle on, pick the charter types the campaign sells, paste the HubSpot form id. The block prefills the destination, reads the from-price from the same departures endpoint the destination page uses, and writes the attribution fields into the lead.

And for an ad that names no destination

On 9 October the team said a campaign may run a generic ad. The form had assumed the page knew the destination; a component should not assume what the page cannot promise. Its default state now asks the destination as the first question, required because HubSpot requires it, from HubSpot's own list; a page that knows its destination passes it and keeps the prefilled line with Change. The pop-up holds until a destination is chosen, the message says what to do and the select takes focus. A second layout, the generic page, exists so the team can preview that state on a phone without the design-system chrome.

Version 1: the smallest page that can take a paid click

Two weeks of evidence produced a hero with four ideas in it: trip questions first, a photo-led phone layout with chips and a sheet, a proof strip beside the ask, and a from-price in the path. Shipping all four at once would have meant never knowing which one earned the result. So the launch decision, taken on 14 October with the team, is to go live with the smallest page that can take a paid click and treat it as the control: one photo, the title, and the full form over it, in its default state with the destination as the first question. No price, because a generic ad has no destination to price. No proof strip yet. The additions the evidence argued for become the test sequence, one variable at a time, each with a baseline to beat.

Hypotheses in full

H1. Trip questions first, contact details last, in two steps. Evidence: F10, F9. Lever: step 1 asks charter type, month and cabins with the destination prefilled; step 2 asks name, email, phone and consent in a pop-up (dialog on desktop, sheet on phone). Metric: submissions per session; step-1 start rate as the leading signal. Constraint: all nine fields posted with their HubSpot values. Confidence: high on direction, untested on this site.

H2. Price in the path. Evidence: F7, F3, F10. Lever: the hero carries the from-price and step 1 ends in "See my price", which reveals a price band before the contact fields. Metric: submissions per session and sales-accepted rate. Constraint: Gareth on lead ownership; the live band would read the departures endpoint. Confidence: medium.

H3. Fewer required fields. Destination prefilled; phone kept but last, with a reason; optional if sales agrees. Applied to every variant.

H4. Photo-led hero. Evidence: F3, F4, the hero-mobile exploration decision. Lever: gallery first, chips and one CTA inside the 665 px fold, form as a sheet. Metric: form start rate and engagement rate. Confidence: medium-high.

H5. Sticky bar on phone. The live page's sticky bar takes more taps than the inline button (F3 rows 2 and 4). Applied to every variant.

Instrumentation, 14 events and 6 hidden fields

Events, snake case, no personal data: lp_view, form_step1_start, form_step1_change, form_step1_complete, form_price_view, form_step2_open, form_field_error, form_submit_success (the only key event), form_submit_error, cta_click, bmq_click, gallery_interact, scroll_depth, phone_click.

Hidden HubSpot fields: utm_source, utm_medium, utm_campaign, utm_content, gclid and fbclid, landing_page_url, lead_source = Paid landing page, page_variant, ga_client_id. The theme already writes the GA client id into a googleid field; nothing else exists yet. Full table with triggers, params and owners in instrumentation.md.

Measurement plan arithmetic

Baseline 3%, lift 30% relative (3% to 3.9%), 80% power, two-sided 5%: n per arm = (1.96 × √(2 × 0.0345 × 0.9655) + 0.84 × √(0.03 × 0.97 + 0.039 × 0.961))² ÷ 0.009² ≈ 6,500 sessions. Two arms need 13,000 sessions: about four weeks if the BVI page's paid landing traffic (about 3,000 users a week) moves here, ten days if the Meta campaigns (about 9,300 a week) do too, four months on the BVI search campaigns alone (about 800 a week), which is not a test. Stop rules: read only at the planned end, minimum two weekly cycles, stop early only if a guardrail breaks by more than 10% relative. Full plan in measurement-plan.md.
