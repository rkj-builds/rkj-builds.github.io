What's behind it

A practice, not medicine

Two things in the room have good evidence behind them. Two are things I enjoy. This says which is which.

Nature soundWater and birdsong help people recover from stress: a meta-analysis across studies (Buxton 2021), recovery after a stressor in the lab (Alvarsson 2010), birdsong on its own (Stobbe 2022).
Slow breathingAbout six breaths a minute, the out-breath longer than the in, has measurable effects on heart-rate variability and reported calm (Zaccaro 2018). The room breathes four seconds in, six out.
Bowls and a gongInstruments, made in your browser. They mark the breath and give the ear something to follow. No frequency here heals anything.
The headphones layerA binaural beat, 10 hertz easing to 6, very quiet, in each ear. The evidence for entrainment is mixed; it is here as an experiment I enjoy, off unless you switch it on.

This is a practice, not medicine. If you are unwell, see someone who can help.

After film school, where I studied sound, I wanted to build a place you could enter from anywhere and feel present in. This is a small version of it, left here for anyone who needs two minutes.

Field recordings: BigSoundBank, CC0. The room was painted with ChatGPT from my brief and cut into 27 layers; the journey runs in your browser, and nothing is recorded or stored.
